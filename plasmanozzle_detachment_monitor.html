Kinetic & Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Formulation

Governing plasma detachment mechanisms in open divergent magnetic topologies (VASIMR and Helicon thrusters).

1. The Alfvèn Critical Detachment Criterion

In an ideal magnetic nozzle, plasma follows magnetic flux lines according to Alfvén's theorem of frozen-in flux. Detachment requires ion kinetic energy density to overwhelm magnetic pressure ($E_{\text{kinetic}} \gg P_{\text{mag}}$), causing the local directed plasma velocity $u_z$ to exceed the local Alfvén speed $v_A$:

$$M_A = \frac{u_z}{v_A} = \frac{u_z \sqrt{\mu_0 \rho_{\text{ion}}}}{\|\mathbf{B}\|} > 1.0$$

When the Alfvén Mach number $M_A < 1$, plasma remains magnetically tethered. Divergent field lines bend back toward the spacecraft magnetic dipole, causing plasma to curl into closed loops and back-stream onto thruster walls, cancelling forward thrust ($F_{\text{net}} \to 0$).

2. Two-Fluid Electron Inertial Separation

Because heavy ions ($m_i \gg m_e$) demagnetize early when ion Larmor radius exceeds magnetic gradient length scale ($r_{L,i} > L_B$), unneutralized electric space-charge fields ($\mathbf{E}_z$) arise to drag magnetized electrons across field lines:

$$L_B = \left| \frac{B_z}{\partial B_z / \partial z} \right| \le r_{L,i} = \frac{m_i u_\perp}{q_i B_z}$$

The ambipolar potential $\phi(z) = \frac{k_B T_e}{e} \ln\left(\frac{n_e(z)}{n_0}\right)$ pulls electrons downstream, maintaining quasi-neutrality. If electron cross-field resistivity is insufficient, electron back-streaming occurs, setting up severe recombination losses along the magnetic margins.

3. Real-Time Abel Inversion & Observer Model

The PlasmaNozzle software processes chord-integrated phase shifts $\Delta \Phi(y)$ from an 8-channel orthogonal laser interferometer, reconstructing radial plasma density $\rho(r, z)$ via regularized matrix inversion:

$$\Delta \Phi(y) = -\frac{e^2 \lambda}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 m_e c^2} \int_{-x_0}^{x_0} n_e(x, y)\, dx \implies \rho(r) = -\frac{1}{\pi} \int_r^R \frac{d\Phi/dy}{\sqrt{y^2 - r^2}}\, dy$$

Comparing reconstructed density gradients $\nabla \rho$ against Hall-probe magnetic gradient $\nabla \|\mathbf{B}\|$ detects peripheral density ridges. A positive correlation in the outer margins indicates immediate detachment breakdown before macroscopic thrust loss manifests.

4. Fast Transient Field Line Reconnection ("Snap Coil")

When the observer detects $M_A$ approaching 1.0 from above, the controller drives a sharp microsecond current pulse ($dI/dt > 10^7\,\text{A/s}$) into downstream tertiary cancellation coils, creating an X-point magnetic null:

$$\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}(r, z, t) = \mathbf{B}_{\text{nozzle}}(r, z) + \mu_0 N I_{\text{snap}}(t) \mathbf{g}(r, z) \implies \left. \mathbf{B}_{\text{total}} \right|_{z_X} = 0$$

Forced resistive reconnection severs the closed magnetic loop, shedding the plasma plasmoid into deep space at exhaust velocity ($v_e > 50\,\text{km/s}$), preventing any momentum loss to the thruster spacecraft bus.
